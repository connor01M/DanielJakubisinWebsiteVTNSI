    Overview

    Daniel Jakubisin's research sits at the intersection of communication theory, signal processing, and machine learning for wireless systems that need to remain effective under interference, delay, uncertainty, and contested spectrum conditions.

    Publicly visible work through Google Scholar, DBLP, IEEE Xplore, and Virginia Tech profiles shows a clear progression from iterative receivers and probabilistic detection to current work in distributed MIMO, RF machine learning, resilient 5G and 6G systems, and underwater acoustic communications.

    Current Research Areas

    • Resilient wireless communications
    • Distributed MIMO and 6G communication systems
    • Probabilistic signal processing and iterative receivers
    • RF machine learning for interference suppression and classification
    • Physical-layer enhancements for contested spectral environments
    • Underwater acoustic communications under interference and jamming

    Background

    Jakubisin is currently a Research Associate Professor in the Spectrum Dominance Division at the Virginia Tech National Security Institute, with a courtesy appointment in the Bradley Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering.

    Previously, he was a technical advisor with a U.S. law firm supporting telecommunications intellectual property and patent matters. He holds a Ph.D. and an M.S. in Electrical Engineering from Virginia Tech, and a B.S. in Electrical Engineering from UNC Charlotte.

    Across the publication record, the recurring theme is reliability: how to keep communication systems working when channels are noisy, interference is strong, or the operating environment is unusually demanding.
